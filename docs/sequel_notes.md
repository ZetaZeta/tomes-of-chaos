# Sequel design notes: overworld magic game (running file)

Purpose: ideas gathered while building Tomes of Chaos that would matter for a sequel with an overworld layer
(cities and nations at war, leaders with agendas, a wandering wizard collecting spellbooks, a home tower, magical research).
Append to this file every wave. Not a commitment for the current game.

## 1. Core premise and loop
- Two layers: a strategic overworld (factions, settlements, armies, events on a clock) and tactical battles (this game's engine, reused as the "resolution layer" for duels, dungeons, sieges and ritual-site fights).
- Player loop: explore, find books and teachers, return to the tower, research, prepare a loadout, go out again. The world keeps moving while you research (research takes time; time is a resource).
- The current game's deck-like spell-per-cast economy (a spell is erased when cast, restored by Restore effects) could become the "prepared spells" system: the tower is where you prepare and restore.

## 2. Spell tiers by scale (the current spell list already hints at three)
- Combat spells (this game): resolved on a tactical board in turns.
- Rituals: take days or weeks, need components/sites/allies, can be interrupted, and have world effects (weather, harvests, plague, wards over a city, curses on a lineage). Wishes, Earthquake, Volcano, Whirlpool, Gentle Repose, Magnificent Mansion, Teleportation Circle, Rope Trick, Simulacrum, Geas and Dominate Person are all natural ritual or world-scale versions.
- Strategic spells: cast at the overworld scale on a settlement, army or leader (scrying, blessing a harvest, ward over a city, mass sleep on an army, curse on a king's heir, summon a storm over a fleet).
- Idea: every tactical spell gets an overworld "shadow" (what it does at scale), stated in data so one spell definition serves both layers.

## 3. Magic acquisition and research
- Books as items: each spellbook has a school, level range, condition, owner history (UA: Bibliomancers care about edition, signed copies, provenance; Cliomancers about historic sites). Provenance and rarity can drive value, theft, and plot.
- Learning: from books (study time), teachers (favours or quests), experimentation (random magic then formalise: UA "five successful random casts then script a ritual"), patrons and gods (Spire divine spells need a patron and rites), pacts with fiends (existing Infernal Pact / Pit Fiend spells).
- Formalising spells: UA rule that more formulas make spontaneous magic harder (a rigidity penalty beyond ~15). A "fixed list vs improvisation" tension suits a spellbook limit: the more you fix, the less you can improvise.
- Failure modes to simulate: unnatural phenomena after botched rituals, wild magic (the existing chaos school), curses that scar a spell permanently.
- Research as a time/cost project with progress bars, rival researchers, and breakthroughs that add new mechanics (the mechanics in this game, such as hung spells, contingencies and mounts, could be unlockable "techniques" rather than spells).

## 4. Resource systems worth modelling
- Charges: minor / significant / major (UA). Generated by deeds, locations or sacrifices, not by magic itself; spent on spells. Good strategic hook: the player gains power by doing things in the world (visit a famous site, own rare books, win a duel, perform a ritual with allies).
- Taboos: breaking a personal rule drains stored power (UA). Gives characters build identity, and gives enemies things to exploit.
- Stress/fallout (Heart, Spire): marking stress to cast, with lasting fallout. Could map to HP, sanity and reputation tracks; casting costs something other than the spell slot.
- Reputation, Shadow, Silver (Spire): faction-facing resources that spells can spend or restore. Matches a political layer.
- Material components and sacred sites: ritual spells need locations (leylines, temples, famous battlefields). Overworld map nodes with magical properties (UA Cliomancy sites, Heart landmarks).
- Familiars, steeds, servants, trapped souls and simulacra as persistent companions across battles (this game has mounts, soul gems, copies and timed summons: promote the useful ones to permanent followers with upkeep).

## 5. Overworld simulation hooks
- Factions with agendas (conquest, trade, faith, survival), each with leaders, resources, mood, and a visible plan the player can learn about through divination.
- Magic as a lever on the simulation: blessing, cursing, scrying, charming a leader (Dominate, Geas, Crave/Glutton's Curse), rumour magic (UA Cliomancy Gnostic Gossip / Urban Legend), forgetting, false identity (Personamancy masks), forecasting (Plutomancy), Urbanomancy riots and "turf". These social and informational spells were mostly skipped for the tactical game but are exactly the overworld spells.
- Information as a resource: scrying, Read Between the Lines (true/false of one fact), Streetwise, divination of omens (Crimson Mirror: three omens that will come up). Fog of war on the overworld that divination lifts.
- Other wizards as rival factions: they raid libraries, steal books (UA Book Theft), race to rituals, and ally or betray.
- Demons and entities: summoning has world cost (UA Demonology: everything nearby dies unless focused through a conduit). Great for high-risk, high-reward rituals.

## 6. Systems the tactical engine already has that carry over
- Statuses with timers, charge-based wards, contingencies, hung spells, mimic, copy, soul trapping, possession, body swap, rewind, temporal stasis, mounts, replicating summons, terrain-changing hazards (lava, chasm), objects with pulses and cycles. These are a rich vocabulary for "what a spell does on a battlefield node".
- Data-driven spells with an automated dominance scanner: keep this. For the sequel, extend it to flag overworld spells that are strictly cheaper or stronger.
- Text generation from data: spell descriptions are built from effect dicts. Keep this so the sequel's two layers stay in sync.

## 7. Open design questions to settle later
- Real-time with pause, or turn-based days on the overworld?
- How much of the spell list survives? (Probably the 700+ tactical spells become "techniques", with a smaller set of rituals added.)
- Permadeath of the wizard vs. succession (apprentice, clone, soul in a gem, Magic Jar into a new body).
- Does the tower have defenders, wards and rooms (Mansion, Rope Trick, Teleportation Circle, Sanctum) that are built from spells?
- Legacy: a persistent world across runs (cities remember what you did) vs. fresh seeds each run.

## CoE5 review (spreadsheet: spells, rituals, magic items)
Sequel-relevant ideas:
- **Rituals are a separate layer from battle spells.** Cast on the overworld, cost a resource (gold, gems of a type, lifeforce, sacrifices, relics, mirrors, "hand of glory"), often need a location (own fortress, a site type, a terrain), and have a binding/failure chance. Good template for the tower's research actions.
- **Resource zoo as a magic constraint**: each school wants a *different* currency (blood->sacrifices, divination->mirrors, alchemy->gems, necromancy->relics/lifeforce). Forces the wizard to route the wandering around specific sites.
- **Rituals split into**: summon (bound creature joins army), commander-making (immortality, ascension, undead conversion), site/fortress enchantment (permanent defensive effect in a city), terrain-changing (turn forest->swamp, etc.), world-scale (affects every province or every enemy of a type; the "apocalypse" tier), political (change a leader's mind, sow unrest, bribe), and portal networks (fast travel between owned sites).
- **Hidden places / scrying**: maps of other planes grant "almost perfect knowledge" of an area on use: items as fog-of-war removers for the overworld.
- **Magic items as a second spell slot**: items summon at start of battle, summon every round (crown of bones, ring of the dead), cast a spell once per battle, carry "xEffects" on weapons (stun, sleep, decay, net, curse), or grant path boosts. Curse tags (cursed items with big upsides and one real drawback: lost eye, chest wound, MR penalty, diseased) are a nice risk/reward lever for a collector wizard.
- **Rebate**: an item that discounts rituals of one school by 20%. Tower upgrades could do the same.
- **Item-only spell scrolls** by level (grants one level-1/2/3 spell on use): the scroll as the loot form of spellbook pages.
- **Cursed gear and a "regain sanity" counter** pair with the sanity-cost note from earlier waves.
- Several spells are marked "takes 2 rounds to cast", "cannot be cast underground", "only affects enemy units", "doesn't affect lifeless": the same qualifiers we already model, but "underground/indoors" as a casting restriction is new and fits overworld sites.

## Wave 22 notes (Vancian supplement, Complete Arcane)
- **Concentration is a core overworld/battle concept.** One held spell at a time; the wizard's attention is the resource. Tower research could unlock "second thread" (Finding the Center) as a permanent upgrade.
- **Vancian memory**: in Vance, a cast spell is *wiped* from the mage's mind until re-memorised: it is the model our "cast erases one copy" already follows. The overworld version: memorising spells at the tower, carrying only what fits in the mind; Gygax's articles stress that the system is a trade between power and helplessness.
- **True names**: Binding of True Names (speak the name, then chant a command in verse) suggests learning an enemy's name as an overworld action that unlocks single-target overrides in battle.
- **Wu jen elements and taboos** (Complete Arcane): five-element affinity (wood/fire/earth/metal/water) and taboos that grant power if kept: a ready-made specialisation system.
- **Mage contracts**: Wrack of the Recalcitrant Spirit (prepare a document with a named being's name for 5000 sp): a ritual-with-cost template; pair with CoE5's resource-per-school idea.
- **Elocation of Ka / Heart of Stone**: storing life force or heart elsewhere = overworld immortality quest ("find the phylactery").
- **Aging as a cost** (Slow Hour): spells that cost years; a lifespan clock for the wandering wizard.

## Wave 23 notes (Noita, Dragon compendium, Invisible Sun, Errant)
- **Noita**: material coatings and reactions (Wet/Oiled/Powdered) are built in the tactics game. For the sequel, a wand-sequencing system is an option: modifiers, triggers and timers chained on a "wand" that the wandering wizard rebuilds in the tower. Orbiting globes, black holes and bloodlust recoil are unbuilt ideas.
- **Dragon 309 "War Spells"**: army-scale spells (Cavalry Call, Small Stronghold, Teleport Legion, Battle Fury, Easy March, Feed the Many, Horde, Herd) belong on the overworld between cities and nations.
- **Planar binding** (Exaction, Dolor, Ensnarement, Implore, Imbrue): a negotiation minigame with bound outsiders and their agendas.
- **Invisible Sun**: practice types (spells, cantrips, charms, signs, hexes, rituals, enchantments, invocations, incantations, processes); the Sooth Deck, which shifts how magic works after significant events; secrets that bend rules; one-use ephemera; spell cost drawn from a stat pool; currencies (Acumen, Joy, Despair, Crux).
- **Errant RPG**:
  - Grimoires as conduits with a learn requirement (a deed, not XP). A grimoire gives a spell of a rolled essence × sphere, built by the player. Each grimoire carries three miscasts and three themes. Good fit for the wizard collecting books.
  - Prepared vs read-from-book casting: reading costs more actions and is once per day; prepared sorceries can turn "unstable" and miscast.
  - Maleficence: burn unstable prepared spells for a signature-damage blast (+1d6 per spell burned).
  - Retorts: reaction casts in answer to an enemy spell. Could become a tactics-game reaction mechanic.
  - Eyes of Marut: capture enemy spells into marble orbs that release when shattered. Candidate for a later spell-capture mechanic.
  - Rituals: offer grimoires (consumed) and permanent favour, then a success tracker over downtime turns (2d6, 3 failures end it). A fit for tower research.
  - Zealot relics: blade, wand, talisman, chalice, spending favour.

## Wave 24-25 notes (Rifts, Guild Wars, Runers, Magic of Faerûn)
- **Runers rune components**: spells built from 1-3 slotted components (element, shape, rider); spell level = sum of component levels (cap 9); discoverable recipes; debuffs that lower a component's level. Fits the wandering wizard's spellbook and tower research.
- **Magic of Faerûn**: spellfire (absorb spells into a stored energy pool, spend as damage or healing), a parallel magic source with its own rules, spellpools (borrow-and-repay spell debt), high magic and mythals (multi-participant rituals, persistent city wards), gem magic (spells stored in gems with triggers), symbol variants that burn prepared spells, fey crossroads portal network, spelldancer (channel turns to unlock cheaper metamagic), energy substitution, faction-gated divine spells.
- **Guild Wars**: totems as stationary aura summons and attack-rider charges are now in the tactics game. For the sequel, hex-count scaling and ritualist spirit bonds suit faction leaders' retinues.
- **Rifts**: ley lines (storms, shutdown, restoration), dances and totem magic, golem creation, planar travel — all map to the overworld magic system.
- Unbuilt tactics mechanics: tile-types Glass; one-way walls; pickup objects (refund spells, contested buffs); spell-failure chance statuses; wall-bounce projectiles.

## Sequel outline (from the user, Oct 2026) — keep this section current
**Layers:** (1) grand strategy where nations interact; (2) town/city layer where the player wanders, explores and adventures; (3) town-and-conversation interfaces for leaders and major people; (4) tactical combat, with battlefield size scaling to the number of enemies; (5) crafting, alchemy and ritual systems.
**Core combat decision:** the player controls only the wizard, never summons or allies directly. This keeps large battles manageable and puts the focus on magic. Allies and summons act under AI, so they need readable orders (Bark Orders / Gestalt-style command spells fit here) and good default behaviour.
**Inspirations:** Dominions 6 (nations, mage-led armies, research, spell paths; a source of ingested spells), Inindo: Way of the Ninja (SNES: wandering a historical world, clan politics, recruiting, conversation-driven play), Ars Magica (technique-and-form magic, spontaneous vs. formulaic casting, covenants, laboratory work; also a source of ingested spells).
**Implications noted:**
- Without direct control of summons, summon spells become "raise a force and set its orders" spells; the squad unit idea (one AI-run group instead of 40 bodies) is now close to required.
- The wizard becomes the whole interface for battle, so spell variety, positioning and mana/cast economy carry the tactics.
- Dominions-style research and Ars Magica-style lab work map to the crafting/ritual layer; Inindo-style town wandering and recruiting maps to the exploration layer.
- Ars Magica's technique + form split (verb x noun) could be used to organise spells and let players invent variants.
- Interim decision: do not build any of this yet; finish the current game first.

## Wave 50 notes
- The `free` (uncontrolled) summon is the model for the sequel's whole ally system: once the wizard commands only himself, every summon, hireling and war band acts like these. Useful extension: a small command vocabulary (hold, follow, charge) set by spells rather than by clicking units.
- Innate statuses and innate auras ("st:" abilities) are a cheap way to give monsters and, later, nation units unit traits that dispel and counter-spells can interact with. Good basis for Dominions-style unit abilities.
- Tough-monster gating by objective could generalise to "enemy composition rules per mission type" on the strategy map: the sequel's scenario generator should pick enemies to pressure the spell list the player has actually built.
- Pestilences: spreading afflictions that copy remaining time are a natural fit for a strategy layer (plague moves between armies and cities along the same adjacency rule). Quarantine, healers' orders and plague-resistant nations could all use the same flag.

## Wave 55 (Academagia dump): what it means for the sequel

Only five tactical spells came out of it (see Addendum 32 of the classification report), but the file is a rich model of several sequel layers.

**[R] Spell invention from phemes.** Every spell is a short list of 3-5 phemes (e.g. Fire, Fire, Rend, Unleash). A pheme has a Difficulty, possible effects (what it contributes) and Spell Types it counts toward with a Magnitude (Divide: Divide 3, Opposition, Multiply 2, Thought 2, Numeric 1). The spell's schools/types come from summing pheme magnitudes. Phemes are unlocked by skill levels and studied separately. This is almost exactly the "technique x form, inputs shape the result" system the spec describes: our `Fx` effects are the phemes, the delivery shape (bolt, burst...) is the technique. Proposal: invention picks 3-5 phemes (from tomes, treasures, creatures studied); each pheme maps to one Fx plus a cost and school weights; the player chooses phemes but not final numbers (the spec's "broad but not fully controlled"). Academagia also lets duplicated phemes stack (Fire, Fire, Lightning, Lightning in Column of the Heavens).
**[R][P] Aetherial materials.** Five cubes (Basic, Neutral, Acidic, Poisonous, Ruinous) are the base ingredient of potions and draughts; Revnk's Reversal converts "wasted" Ruinous into Basic. This is a ready model for per-school natural treasures as crafting inputs, and for the idea that waste products can be refined back.
**[R] Items and Artifice.** Items have type, durability (durable N or perishable N days), size (encumbrance), concealability (professors confiscate items above your Conceal skill), worth, abilities, and a recipe (ingredients, quality, an Artificing skill, a Finishing skill, an Enchant skill). Items can be unidentified: trial and error risks vitality or stress, a registry costs money, a spell identifies them safely (and better on a high roll). Shops come in three kinds (catalogue: delay, no selling; merchant: buy and sell; shop: sell only). Useful for the town layer [D] and tower crafting.
**[C] Duels.** Spells and actions have a duel cost; duelling range is a number the spells can change (Angular Displacement adds range). A separate rule set for formal one-on-one magical duels between rivals, sitting beside open battle.
**[K][D] Probabilistic social engine.** Rolls are "Attribute/Skill vs difficulty", with rerolls as a resource (Charm of the Les'enik, Seven Favors), chance of success/failure/discovery as modifiers on whole categories of action, stress as a resource that limits what you can do, relationships (-N to +N between any two characters) and cliques. These are the real mechanics of the town and diplomacy layer; our tactical combat is deterministic, so the sequel's town layer needs its own roll model and should not borrow the combat one.
**[K] Skills.** Parent skills with subskills, "steps" of improvement, Abilities as permanent passives unlocked by skill levels, Actions as scheduled calendar activities (the calendar of a day with thirds). Useful reference for a time-slice model on the overworld/tower layers.
**[S] Mastery.** The forbidden school (Master, Puppet Master) with permanent ethics costs: a ready example of a school that some nations ban and whose spells cost reputation (ties into the spec's school bans).

**[D] Level 1 as a teaching tier (wave 56).** Every level 1 attack now carries a secondary effect, and 18 small control, defence and combo spells exist at level 1 (Hesitant, Aversion, Forgetful, Jostled, Riposte, Overlooked, Anchored, Gummed, Slick, Parched, Soaked, Prone). The sequel can reuse this pattern: introduce each status as a weak level 1 spell, then scale it up at higher levels.

- **Gate objects that summon (from the tactics game):** an object with a cycle can release creatures for its owner each turn (Pandemonium Gate: one rogue tier 4 creature a turn for five turns). Streaming a payoff over several turns balanced a drawback-heavy spell far better than an instant burst; reuse for overworld "portals" or ongoing summoning sites.

- **Damage types with a niche (from the tactics game):** a type that only works on some targets (holy: undead and fiends) is fine for spells but wrecks creatures whose whole attack uses it. Give the creature a broad cousin (radiant: hits all, +1 to the unholy). Also: probe the AI per spell (best score in a typical position); spells the bot never casts look weak in balance data and are better fixed in the bot than in the spell.
